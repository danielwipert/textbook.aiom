# Stage 2 developmental check: AI Operations Management, Chapter 2

**You are the independent check on a developmental edit.** Read the files in the
order given and answer the questions below.

- **`AIOM_Ch02_prose_for_review.md`** is the chapter. Read it FIRST, and read it
  the way the target reader would, straight through and once.
- **`ch02_reader_model_extract.md`** is who that reader is. Read it before the
  chapter.
- **`AIOM_Ch02_Stage2_findings.md`** is the review you are checking. **Read it
  LAST**, after you have written your own answers to questions 1 through 5. Do not
  look at it early. The whole value of this exercise is that your reading is
  uncontaminated by the drafter's.

**The chapter's two figures are not reproduced.** Each is marked in place and its
caption is kept, so you can see where a figure sits and what it shows. The drawn
geometry is not available to you and no finding should depend on it.

## Why you are being asked

This chapter was drafted by the same model that then ran the developmental edit on
it. **A chapter judged by the model that drafted it is self-marking**, and this
project has already paid for that once on this chapter: a bias review found a
structural defect, that the chapter's own craft diagnostic failed a test its section
2.2 sets, which the drafting model had not seen in its own work.

You are the check that replaces nothing and adds what the drafter cannot supply,
which is a first reading. **The drafter has read this chapter perhaps thirty times
and can no longer be surprised by it.** You can. That is the instrument.

## The one question this step asks

**Does this chapter teach?** Not whether it is true, which is Stage 3, and not
whether it is well written, which is Stage 4. Concretely: clarity, pacing, cognitive
load, example fitness, transitions, and whether the argument carries the target
reader to its conclusion without a stall.

Answer these five before reading the review.

1. **Where did you first stall?** Name the paragraph, quote its opening sentence,
   and say what you had to do to recover: reread, look back at an earlier section,
   or carry an unexplained term forward. **If you did not stall, say so plainly and
   say where the chapter was working hardest to prevent it.** This is the question
   the drafter structurally cannot answer about its own chapter, so answer it in
   full before you answer any other.
2. **What is the chapter's peak cognitive load, and is it in the right place?**
   Count what a reader is holding at once at the heaviest point. Say whether the
   chapter gives them anything to hold it with, and whether the load arrives before
   or after the artifact that would reduce it.
3. **Is each example the right example for the job it does?** The chapter carries
   an opening case, a worked illustration in the teaching body, two mappings in the
   craft section, and four problems. For each, state what it is teaching and whether
   a reader who understood the preceding prose learns anything from it. **Name any
   two that do the same work.**
4. **Where does a transition fail?** Quote the last sentence of one section and the
   first of the next, at the two places where the chapter changes subject most
   sharply, and rule whether a reader knows why the second follows the first.
5. **What would you cut, and what is missing?** The chapter is roughly 7,250 words
   across eight teaching sections. Name anything that could be removed with no loss
   to a reader's ability to run the diagnostic, and anything a reader needs that is
   not there.

## Then, and only then, read the review you are checking

`AIOM_Ch02_Stage2_findings.md` carries nine findings, DE1 through DE9, three of them
marked HIGH.

6. **Which of its findings do you disagree with, and why?** A finding you would rule
   as not a defect is as useful as one you would confirm.
7. **What did it miss?** This is the question you were brought in for. Compare your
   answers to 1 through 5 against the nine findings and name what has no counterpart
   there.
8. **Is any finding overstated in a way that would cause unnecessary rework?** This
   is not a formality. **Two of the candidate remedies in that document re-run the
   structural gate on the whole chapter, and any change to body prose re-runs four
   later steps.** A finding that is real but minor, acted on, costs more than it
   returns. Say which of the nine you would rule as recorded and not acted on.
9. **Rank the nine by what a reader actually loses.** The drafter ranked them HIGH,
   MEDIUM and LOW. Give your own ranking and say where it differs.

## What is out of bounds

Findings in these categories will be discarded, and saying so costs you nothing.

- **Fact and sourcing.** Stage 3 has not run. Every figure and date in the chapter
  is formally unverified and the chapter says so in its own provenance line.
- **Voice, register, word choice and sentence-level style.** Stage 4 owns these and
  has not run. The chapter also works under prohibitions you have not been given.
  **Do not suggest replacement wording for anything.**
- **Structure, meaning which slots exist and in what order.** Stage 1 closed on
  2026-08-24 and its findings were ruled by the book's author. Four rulings are
  settled and are not reopened here: that section 2.3 is kept and its review cadence
  moves to Chapter 10; that problem P3 maps a cited real deployment rather than a
  constructed one; that the chapter's treatment of adoption and value is correct as
  it stands; and that the anchor theorem arriving in section 2.7, after the argument
  it anchors, is not a defect. **Finding DE9 concerns section 2.3's POSITION within
  the teaching body and does not reopen the decision to keep it.** You may say a
  section is in the wrong place. Do not say it should not exist.
- **The six-slot skeleton.** Opening case, teaching body, craft section, summary,
  key terms, discussion questions and problems, in that order, in all fifteen
  chapters. It is fixed and it is not a finding.

## Constraints

- **Quote the chapter for every finding.** An unquoted finding cannot be acted on.
- **Prefer a measurement to a judgment.** Word counts, distances between a claim and
  its evidence, and counts of what a reader is holding are checkable. "This feels
  slow" is not.
- **Rank by severity, and say plainly where the chapter is sound.** A review that
  finds only defects gives the author nothing to protect.
- **Say what is wrong, not how to write the replacement.**
