# Chapter 2, Stage 7. External check A: the sources.

**Give this prompt, `AIOM_Ch02_Stage7_render.pdf` and `AIOM_Ch02_Stage7_packet.md` to
one model in its own session. Do not give it check B or check B's output.**

---

You are fact checking one chapter of an academic textbook before it locks. Work to
the standard of a university press fact checker: a claim is verified when you have
seen the document that supports it, not when it sounds right.

**Say up front whether you have live web access in this session, and if you do not,
say so in your first line and treat every "verified" below as unavailable to you.**
The previous checker on this chapter had no web access and disclosed it, which was
the single most useful thing in its report, because it told us how to read
everything that followed. **Do not simulate a lookup you cannot perform.** If you
cannot open a document, the answer is "cannot confirm", never a summary from memory
presented as a check.

## What you have

The PDF is the chapter as it will print. The packet lists every passage carrying a
citation marker, the keys it cites, and the register entry behind each key with its
note reproduced verbatim. The notes carry verification history and, for findings
already ruled, the condition that would reverse the ruling.

## Your task, in priority order

**1. Five specific changes, each on a live citation.** This chapter was copy edited
after its first fact check, and eight passages changed in ways that touch what a
source must support. These five are the ones only a source can settle. For each,
state VERIFIED, CONTRADICTED, or CANNOT CONFIRM, and quote the source language you
relied on.

- The chapter reports monthly spending of "several hundred to **several thousand**
  dollars" per engineer, cited to Forbes. **An earlier draft said "a few thousand".**
  Which does the source support? This is the highest-value item in the check.
- The chapter says the executive "could not connect its growing use of **Claude
  Code** to the consumer features it was producing", cited to Fortune. **Did the
  remark concern Claude Code specifically, or AI spending generally?**
- The chapter asserts directly that "Uber had already exhausted its annual AI budget"
  by April 2026. Does the source support the assertion, the date, and the word
  "exhausted"?
- The chapter says engineers were doing the work "by using the **tools** it had given
  them", plural. Does the reporting describe one tool or several?
- For the Australian trial: the chapter says "The evaluation **identified two limits**
  in its method". Does the evaluation characterize them as limits, or merely state
  them?

**2. The Australian DTA trial, which is the chapter's largest exposure.** It is
graded C, has no location recorded, and nobody has read it. Problem P3 is built
entirely on it. Verify, each separately: the trial ran January to June 2024; around
sixty agencies took part; several thousand licences were issued; agencies nominated
recipients; pricing was per seat rather than per unit of use; the Digital
Transformation Agency commissioned an independent evaluation and published it in
full; participants reported using the tool a few times a week or less; participants
self-assessed effects; no pre-trial baseline measure of the work existed. **Give the
document's own title, author line and location if you can reach it.** If any of these
is contradicted, say which, because the problem then needs rebuilding rather than
rewording.

**3. The MIT NANDA study.** Confirm the wording of the headline finding, the author
line, and whether "MIT NANDA" is the correct name of the publishing body. The chapter
rests its argument on the phrase "no measurable impact" rather than on the
ninety-five per cent figure, so tell us whether that phrase is the report's own.

**4. The two paywalled news sources**, Forbes and Fortune, for everything else the
packet attributes to them, including the roughly 5,000 engineers, the December 2025
rollout, and the direct quotation "That link is not there yet."

## Rules for your findings

- **Number every finding.** Give the exact sentence from the PDF, what you checked it
  against, and your verdict.
- **Separate "the source contradicts this" from "I could not reach the source".** They
  get different rulings and conflating them wastes a round.
- **A proposed remedy is welcome and will be judged separately from the finding.** Two
  house rules constrain remedies and a remedy breaking either will be rejected while
  the finding stands: body prose carries **no hedging language** ("perhaps", "some
  argue", "one might say"), and **no em dashes anywhere**. Accurate qualification is
  not hedging.
- **Do not raise a ruled finding as new.** The packet marks these. If you reach one,
  say whether the condition in its REVERSES-IF is now met.
- **Do not comment on prose quality, structure, or teaching.** Other steps own those.
- **Report the footnote 5 placement defect nowhere.** It is known, it is a pagination
  matter, and it is booked to the design review.
