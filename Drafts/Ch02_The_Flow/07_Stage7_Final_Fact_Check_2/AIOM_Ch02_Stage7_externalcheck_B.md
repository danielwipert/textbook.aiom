# AIOM Chapter 2, Stage 7: External Check B

## Executive summary

This check finds that the chapter's main internal vulnerability is evidentiary overreach in the Uber opening case and its downstream reuse in the craft section and P1. The chapter repeatedly acknowledges that public reporting does not establish Uber's internal spending view, contract terms, provider controls, or buyer-side consumption records, but several later sentences treat those matters as known. The single highest-priority fix is to narrow the sentence claiming that the deployment scaled before Uber had a mechanism to control cost or measure return. The chapter's quantities are mostly safe when they are stipulated examples or problem facts, but the Uber quantities become unsafe when a reported per-engineer cost range is used to diagnose buyer-side record management. The unsupported generalization pattern is concentrated in claims about what organizations usually, commonly, normally, or almost always do with record flows, project funding, adoption data, and first-year deployments.

## 1. Single highest-priority fix

Fix the Uber control conclusion.

Confident sentence: "It does show that the deployment scaled before the company had a mechanism to control its cost or measure its return." Section: Opening case, p. 3.

Careful sentences: "Public reporting does not reveal whether Uber had such a view or what its finance team could see." Section: Opening case, p. 2. "The public account does not establish what the contract priced, what consumption data Uber could see or when it became available, or whether the provider offered spending limits or alerts that the company declined." Section: Opening case, p. 3.

Wrong conclusion permitted: A reader would conclude that the public account proves Uber lacked cost controls and return measurement. The chapter elsewhere says the public account does not establish Uber's spending visibility, consumption data, provider alerts, provider limits, or contract terms.

Narrow remedy: "It shows that the deployment scaled before any public account identified a control that changed the course of spending or a measure that connected use to return."

Rank: Critical.

## 5. Named company, product, executive, title, and quotation audit

1. Company: Uber.

   Opening: "By April 2026, just four months into the year, Uber had already exhausted its annual AI budget." Opening case, p. 1.

   Later uses: "Uber's experience makes the problem from Chapter 1 concrete." Opening case, p. 3. "Uber's position in the opening case shows this first error in practice." Section 2.6, p. 13. "Applied to Uber on the evidence publicly reported..." Craft section, p. 18.

   Consistency result: The company name is consistent. The issue is not identity. The issue is evidentiary strength: later mapping statements overstate what the public account establishes about Uber's internal records and controls.

2. Product: Claude Code.

   Opening: "The tool was Claude Code, an AI assistant that writes and edits software." Opening case, p. 1.

   Later opening use: "depending on how often each engineer used Claude Code and the complexity of the work." Opening case, p. 2. "connect its growing use of Claude Code to the consumer features it was producing." Opening case, p. 3.

   Consistency result: The product name is internally consistent. Later references to "the tool" are clear. The phrase "using the tools it had given them" on p. 1 shifts briefly to the plural, but the surrounding paragraph identifies Claude Code unambiguously. This is not reported as a finding.

3. Executive: Andrew Macdonald.

   Quote and title: "Yet president and chief operating officer Andrew Macdonald said the company still could not connect its growing use of Claude Code to the consumer features it was producing. 'That link is not there yet,' he said." Opening case, p. 3.

   Later references: "as the chief operating officer's question suggests." Craft section, p. 18. "the gap behind the chief operating officer's question." P1, p. 24.

   Quotation: "That link is not there yet." Opening case, p. 3.

   Consistency result: The first paraphrase is narrower than the second. The quote is about a link between Claude Code use and consumer features. The later paraphrase moves to "any documented return," which is broader than consumer features. This is included in finding 9.

## 7. Bottom line

The chapter's main internal risk is not the three-flow framework. It is the Uber case being made to carry more than the chapter says public reporting can bear. The critical fixes are to narrow Uber's control, contract, spending-visibility, record-flow, and value-measurement claims to what the public account shows.

If only one sentence can be fixed, fix this one: "It does show that the deployment scaled before the company had a mechanism to control its cost or measure its return." Replace it with: "It shows that the deployment scaled before any public account identified a control that changed the course of spending or a measure that connected use to return."
