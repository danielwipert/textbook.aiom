# Bias check: AI Operations Management, Chapter 2

**You are given two files. Read both, then answer the questions below.**

- **`AIOM_Ch02_prose_for_review.md`** is the chapter under review.
- **`AIOM_Ch01_extract_for_comparison.md`** is the previous chapter, locked and
  published. **You are not reviewing it.** It is the standard, and it is included
  so that question 1 can be answered with quotations from both.

## Why you are being asked

This chapter of a management textbook opens on a real episode in which
**Uber exhausted its 2026 AI budget four months into the year after Anthropic's
Claude Code spread across roughly 5,000 engineers.**

The chapter was drafted by Claude, which is Anthropic's model. **The chapter is
therefore written about the drafting vendor's own product, and you are the control
for that conflict.** The author of the book, Dan Wipert, ruled the case in on the
condition that a second model check the prose for bias. You are that check.

You are not being asked whether the chapter is good. You are being asked whether
it is fair.

## What to look for, in both directions

Bias here has two failure modes and the second is as likely as the first.

**Softening.** Language that protects Anthropic or Claude Code. Look for the
overrun framed as a triumph of adoption; the vendor's pricing model presented as
inevitable or neutral when it is a choice; blame placed on the buyer's forecasting
where the sources place it on the arrangement; the tool's usefulness asserted where
the sources only report its use; and any suppressed fact that a neutral writer
would have included.

**Overcorrection.** Criticism the sources do not support, inserted to appear
even-handed. Look for implied vendor culpability, insinuation about intent, and
adjectives doing work the evidence does not.

**Asymmetry against Chapter 1.** Chapter 1 opens on Cursor, a different vendor,
and treats it as follows: the vendor's behavior is derived from the economics, the
vendor is not scolded, the buyer is not mocked, and the correction is presented as
something the provider's cost position required. **Chapter 2 must treat Anthropic
exactly as Chapter 1 treats Cursor and Anysphere.** If the two chapters differ in
warmth, blame, or benefit of the doubt, say so and quote both. **The drafting model
has no relationship with Anysphere and does have one with Anthropic**, which is
what makes this comparison the sharpest instrument you have.

## The seven questions

1. **Quote any sentence** where the treatment of Anthropic or Claude Code differs
   from how a neutral author would treat an unrelated vendor. For each, say which
   direction it errs and propose a replacement.
2. Does the chapter ever imply the overrun reflects well on the tool?
3. Does the chapter place responsibility somewhere the reported facts do not
   support, in either direction?
4. The chapter argues the failure was the buyer's missing record flow rather than
   the vendor's pricing. **Is that framing sound, or is it convenient?** Argue the
   opposing case as strongly as you can, then say which is right.
5. Would you be able to tell, from the prose alone, that the drafting model and the
   product under discussion come from the same company? Point to anything that
   gives it away.
6. Are the qualifications accurate? Reported figures should read as reported, not
   as established. Flag anything stated more confidently than a paywalled
   secondary source supports.
7. **What is missing?** Name any fact a fair account of this episode would include
   that the chapter omits.

## Constraints on your answer

- Quote the chapter's own words for every finding. An unquoted finding cannot be
  acted on.
- Rank findings by severity and say plainly if there are none.
- Do not rewrite for style. A separate craft standard governs the prose and
  restyling will be discarded.
- The source register is deliberately excluded from what you were given. Every
  figure is unverified and a separate fact check owns that. **Judge the framing,
  not the facts.**
