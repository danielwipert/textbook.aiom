## R-3: SUPPORTED (adding S4 for timing)

No problem with "By April 2026". Headline, author and date match.

Source says: "Uber's surging use of AI coding tools, particularly Anthropic's
Claude Code, has maxed out its full year AI budget just a few months into 2026,
according to chief technology officer Praveen Neppalli Naga." and "I'm back to
the drawing board because the budget I thought I would need is blown away
already..."

Timing correction: "just four months into the year" can imply four COMPLETED
months. The report appeared during the fourth month, so it establishes
exhaustion by April 14 without identifying the exact date.

Proposed wording (the case's first sentence):
"By April 14, 2026, Uber had already exhausted its full-year AI budget,
according to its chief technology officer, Praveen Neppalli Naga."

Claude's note: the decision package had listed "just four months" under
"considered, no change". The checker raises it again; it goes to the sitting.

## R-1: CANNOT CONFIRM (final verdict needs S8)

S4 supports the narrower observation that this report identifies no effective
spending intervention before exhaustion. It does not establish that Uber had no
controls internally.

The article does report uses and output: AI-written code supported backend
systems, bug fixes and new features; the CTO said AI agents wrote about 11
percent of live backend code updates, up from a fraction of a percent three
months earlier. Those are evidence of deployment and output. They do not
calculate whether benefits exceeded costs or quantify output attributable to AI
against a baseline. The supervisor agents were a future quality-control plan,
not an implemented spending control.

Proposed wording: hold until S8. Any conclusion about returns should distinguish
reported operational benefits from a measured economic return.
