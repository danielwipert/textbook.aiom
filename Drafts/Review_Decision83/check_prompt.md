## PART 1. Your task

The three chapters below are published and locked. The author has approved ten
small additions: one narrowed sentence, one added sentence and one added
citation in Chapter 2's opening case, and seven new "dated boxes". A dated box
is a short, dated, sourced passage set beside the timeless teaching prose.

The wording was drafted by a model that also read the sources. A draft judged by
its drafter is self-marking, so you are the check. You have two jobs.

### Job 1. Check every proposed sentence against its source

For each item, open the source (or the attachment) and confirm, sentence by
sentence:

- every number, date, name, job title and company is exactly as the source has
  it;
- every quotation is word for word, and attributed to the right speaker;
- the sentence says no more than the source says (an unnamed-source fact is not
  presented as a company statement; a projection is not presented as a result;
  figures with different scopes are not merged);
- nothing important in the source contradicts the sentence.

Do not judge style. Judge only whether the words are true to the source.

### Job 2. A short red-flag sweep of the published chapters

The author's reviewer found nothing in the three chapters that has been
disproven by reporting since they were written. Confirm or contradict that.
Report only MAJOR problems: a cited fact now contradicted, or a general claim
about how AI is bought, priced, consumed or managed that major events have shown
wrong. A dated box being out of date is not a red flag; it is dated on purpose.
If you find nothing major, say so plainly.

Allowed sources for Job 2: the Financial Times, the Wall Street Journal,
Bloomberg, Reuters, The Information, the New York Times, The Economist, and
first-party company material. Do not raise these cases, which later chapters
own: Klarna's correction of its AI claims; the MIT NANDA study; provider pricing
and usage-limit changes; shadow AI; Air Canada's chatbot.
