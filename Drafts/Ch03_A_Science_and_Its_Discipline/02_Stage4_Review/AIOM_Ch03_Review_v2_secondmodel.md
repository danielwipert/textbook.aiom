# Second-model review: Chapter 3 (redesigned)

## Section A: Structure

The main structural risk is that readers can reproduce the five labels without being able to make defensible judgments about an unfamiliar deployment. The audit needs a firmer distinction between an absent capability, a partially performed function, and missing evidence. The boundary argument also asks readers to settle an ownership dispute before showing them how to distinguish overlapping responsibilities. The science layer needs one demonstration of how a conditional claim changes a business judgment, rather than additional explanation of how claims are organized.

## Section B: Teaching

The greatest teaching difficulty arises when the chapter moves from describing activities to judging whether a function exists and who should own it. Those judgments require decision rules that the worked examples currently supply through assertion. The first cut and the most useful additional example are identified below. The chapter needs a redistribution of explanation, not length for its own sake.

## Section C: Voice and craft

Chapter 1 supplies the calibration: its contact-center inventory exposes assumptions, performs the calculation, and explains what the resulting number changes. Its sentence “The existing system did not fail” follows a structural explanation rather than substituting for one. Chapter 3’s weakest passages assert disciplinary necessity or announce conceptual relationships before demonstrating their consequences. The seven criterion findings below pair the weakest passage with stronger evidence from Chapter 3 itself.

## Fix first

1. **S-1:** Correct the distinction between absence and missing evidence before readers adopt it as an audit method.
2. **S-2 and S-13:** Replace categorical boundary claims with a defensible classification rule and one worked ownership decision, so readers can perform Assessment 4.
3. **S-4:** Establish consistent rules for partial functions and for sourcing versus routing, then apply them across the worked audits.
