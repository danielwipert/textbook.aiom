# Fact-check package B: Chapter 3 findings

All quotes verified against the supplied text. Section labels refer to the chapter. Where two sentences conflict I quote both. Per the instructions I do not judge whether any fact is true; every finding below is about a claim exceeding what the chapter itself shows.

---

B-1. Rank 1 [Problems P2 · Step 2]
Sentence: "Choosing which employees receive a license is allocation: agency nomination determines who gets capacity."
Conflicts with: "The allocation and routing function begins only when a stated rule sends different work to different sources of capacity or decides what runs first." [Craft section, Two rules] and "Allocation and routing is not shown: the reporting describes no rule for which work went to the tool or any limit on its heaviest users." [Problems P1 · Step 2/4]
A reader would wrongly conclude: any decision about who receives capacity discharges the allocation and routing function, so an organization that hands out seats has performed it and owes no routing rule; the worked answer also teaches a boundary that P1 then applies the other way, since Uber bought one tool for all engineers and P1 records that as sourcing with allocation not shown.
Proposed remedy: "Buying per-seat licenses is sourcing. Choosing which employees receive a license distributes seats and so belongs to sourcing and to planning; the allocation and routing function begins only when a stated rule decides what runs first or sends different work to different sources of capacity, and agency nomination states no such rule."

B-2. Rank 2 [3.1 The science and the practice]
Sentence: "Both conditions therefore held for a team on that plan."
Conflicts with: "After June 2025, each request drew down a twenty-dollar monthly credit at the model provider's rate, and further use was priced at the same rates." [3.1, same paragraph]
A reader would wrongly conclude: the vendor's price list settles the matter for an actual team, that is, that a team on the plan necessarily bore cost beyond the access price and necessarily had use made visible by measurement, when the chapter has shown only that the plan permits both; a team that never exhausts its credit bears cost equal to the access price.
Proposed remedy: "The plan made both conditions available to a team on it: cost could extend beyond the access price, and the credit drawdown made resource use visible. Whether they held for a given team turns on that team's usage records, which is what Chapter 1's consumption-event inventory supplies."

B-3. Rank 3 [3.3, Table 3.1, FinOps row]
Sentence: "Its 2026 report quotes a practitioner saying that no one can yet answer whether AI is providing value"
Conflicts with: "Asked whether AI was providing value, one practitioner acknowledged the uncertainty. The report quoted the answer: 'No one can answer that question yet.'" [Opening case]
A reader would wrongly conclude: the Foundation's 2026 report states as its position that no one can answer whether AI provides value, when the chapter shows one respondent's answer to one survey question; a reader would also conclude that this statement belongs to the source the table's own footnote cites for the row, the "What is FinOps?" definition page, which the chapter elsewhere cites for the definition of the practice, not for any survey finding.
Proposed remedy: "It grew from managing cloud cost and usage. The Foundation's State of FinOps 2026 quotes one surveyed practitioner answering that no one can answer yet whether AI is providing value."

B-4. Rank 4 [Craft section, closing paragraph]
Sentence: "Using six steps and a few public facts, the audit showed a company changing its entire supply of AI capacity because of cost."
Conflicts with: "In June 2026, he announced that Lindy had moved all its traffic to DeepSeek V4, a model from a Chinese developer, and said the move would save millions of dollars." [Craft section, Lindy box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: the record establishes why Lindy moved providers, when the quoted statements establish only that it moved, that its founder compared model cost with payroll, and that he expected savings; the audit also attributes to itself a causal finding its own Step 4 marks "not shown" for value, and the record does not show the traffic moved back or that the savings were realized.
Proposed remedy: "Using six steps and the public facts quoted above, the audit examined a company whose founder reported that models cost more than payroll and announced a move of all traffic to one provider whose terms he expected to save millions of dollars. The record states the comparison and the expected saving; it does not state the reason for the move beyond those words."

B-5. Rank 5 [Opening case]
Sentence: "Its central finding was how far FinOps had expanded into AI spending: 98 percent of respondents now managed it, up from 31 percent two years earlier."
Conflicts with: "FinOps is a capable neighboring discipline that has expanded into work it was not built to govern: the economics of an organization's AI consumption." [Opening case] and "ing, and by 2026 nearly all respondents to the Foundation's survey were managing it." [Opening case]
A reader would wrongly conclude: the discipline of FinOps has expanded into AI spending, when the only evidence offered is what 1,192 respondents to one organization's survey reported about their own duties; the later restatement drops the figure and the scope word and reports the same fact as "nearly all."
Proposed remedy: "Its central finding concerned its own respondents: 98 percent of them now managed AI spending, up from 31 percent of respondents two years earlier." And for the restatement: "Its practitioners later took on AI spending, and by 2026 the Foundation reported that 98 percent of its survey respondents were managing it."

B-6. Rank 6 [3.3 Where the discipline ends]
Sentence: "It already manages spending, has expanded into AI, and defines its purpose in terms of maximizing the business value of technology."
Conflicts with: "For years, it had described its purpose as 'advancing the people who manage the value of cloud.' The new statement replaced one word: 'advancing the people who manage the value of technology.'" [Opening case] and "Maximizing the business value of technology through financial accountability and collaboration among engineering, finance, and business teams" [Table 3.1, FinOps row, footnoted to the "What is FinOps?" definition page]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that the Foundation's definition of the practice, the source cited in footnote 16, has always named business value of technology, and that FinOps as a discipline has expanded into AI, rather than its surveyed practitioners having taken on AI spend while the Foundation changed one word in its mission statement in February 2026. The chapter's three descriptions of FinOps do not agree on whether the subject of the change is the discipline, the practitioners, or the Foundation's own wording.
Proposed remedy: "It already manages spending, its surveyed practitioners have taken on AI spending, and since February 2026 the Foundation states its purpose as advancing the people who manage the value of technology."

B-7. Rank 7 [3.2, fifth function, next to the JPMorgan box]
Sentence: "At the end of a quarter, the organization should be able to say whether the assistant paid for itself, identifying its costs and returns, the months covered, and the outcome used to judge it."
Conflicts with: "The published accounts do not say which costs, benefits, or period either figure covered." [JPMorgan box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that the box illustrates a bank operating the value-accountability function, when by the chapter's own criteria the function is not shown: the two figures were published after spending began, they disagree by a factor of about three, and no boundary, period, named owner, or quarterly measure appears in the record quoted.
Proposed remedy: "The bank's chief executive and chief financial officer published two different pairings of AI cost and benefit after the spending had begun, and the published accounts do not state the boundary, the period, the outcome, or the person responsible, so under the function audit the public record does not show the fifth function as performed."

B-8. Rank 8 [3.2, fourth function, next to the Microsoft box]
Sentence: "Both require a stated rule that managers can check against the results and change."
Conflicts with: "Reports put the volume at tens of thousands of prompts a week. Routine tasks moved to the in-house models, while complex reasoning tasks could still go to the outside models." [Microsoft box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that Microsoft performs the allocation and routing function as the chapter defines it, when the box reports a stated goal and a pattern of movement, no rule, and no figure for total prompt volume, so the reader cannot tell what share of traffic the change affects.
Proposed remedy: "The box reports a stated goal and a pattern of movement between model families; it reports no rule that managers can check against results and change, and no total prompt volume against which tens of thousands of prompts a week can be sized."

B-9. Rank 9 [Opening case]
Sentence: "The 2026 survey reached 1,192 respondents responsible for more than $83 billion in annual cloud spending."
Conflicts with: "the Foundation's annual survey of its practitioners, the people inside companies who manage cloud spending for a living" [same paragraph] and "These figures describe the survey's respondents and were reported by a vendor of planning software." [Pigment box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that 98 percent of practitioners in general now manage AI spending, when the chapter discloses the frame for the two vendor surveys (Pigment's vendor role, DoiT's commissioning of Sapio Research) but never says how the Foundation's respondents were drawn or that they are self-selected members of the community whose progress the survey reports.
Proposed remedy: "The 2026 survey reached 1,192 respondents drawn from that community of the Foundation's own practitioners, responsible for more than $83 billion in annual cloud spending."

B-10. Rank 10 [Craft section, Lindy audit, Step 4]
Sentence: "The public record contains no forecast of consumption, no rule for which work runs where, and no measure of whether each customer's use pays for itself."
Conflicts with: "He said he would consider returning to Anthropic's models if their prices fell and expected the company to keep spending more on AI than on payroll." [Lindy box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that Step 1 listed everything the box reports, and that the record holds no forward-looking statement about spending at all; the omitted statement is weak evidence for planning and budgeting, but the audit's own Step 4 rule says a public record shows only what a company chose to report, so an activity that appears in the box must appear in the list before the verdict is written.
Proposed remedy: "The record contains no figure of expected consumption for a coming period and no comparison of spending against such a figure; the founder's expectation that spending on AI would continue to exceed payroll is not such a figure."

B-11. Rank 11 [3.2, third function, DoiT box]
Sentence: "Seventy-nine percent reported AI-related cost overruns in the previous twelve months."
Conflicts with: "DoiT, 'Why 79% of Enterprises Overspent on AI in 2026,' DoiT blog, 2026" [footnote 10]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that enterprises overspent on AI in 2026, when the survey conducted in February 2026 covers the twelve months before it, that is, most of 2025, and reports what 500 finance leaders at large organizations in two countries said about overruns, not a measured spend against budget.
Proposed remedy: "Seventy-nine percent reported AI-related cost overruns in the twelve months before the survey's February 2026 fieldwork."

B-12. Rank 12 [3.2, second function, Pigment box]
Sentence: "Thirteen percent said they had stayed within budget, and the average overrun was about 27 percent."
Conflicts with: "These figures describe the survey's respondents and were reported by a vendor of planning software." [same box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that organizations overrun AI budgets by about 27 percent, when the box states neither the base the 27 percent is taken against, nor whether it averages only the respondents who overran, nor the period over which spending was measured; the chapter's rule that every quantity states what it covers is not applied to its own box.
Proposed remedy: "Thirteen percent said they had stayed within budget. Among respondents who reported an overrun, the average was about 27 percent of the spending they had expected; the report does not state the period those figures cover."

B-13. Rank 13 [3.2, first function, next to the Airbnb box]
Sentence: "Requirements may include accuracy, speed, cost per request, and ease of connection to the systems that feed a model."
Conflicts with: "He said the company relied heavily on Alibaba's Qwen models, which he called 'very good' and 'also fast and cheap.' He also said OpenAI's tools for connecting its models to Airbnb's systems were not yet ready to meet the company's needs." [Airbnb box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that the box documents all four requirements as Airbnb's selection criteria, when the quoted account names quality, speed, and price, and mentions connection tools only as a deficiency at one provider, not as a requirement Airbnb weighed; a later reader applying Step 1 of the audit to this record would carry the fourth requirement in on the box's authority.
Proposed remedy: "Requirements may include accuracy, speed, and cost per request, as the Airbnb account illustrates, and ease of connection to the systems that feed a model, which that account mentions only as a shortfall in one provider's tools."

B-14. Rank 14 [Problems P1 · Step 1]
Sentence: "Reported spending ranged from $150 to $250 a month for the average engineer and from $500 to $2,000 for the heaviest users."
Conflicts with: "rolling it out to roughly 5,000 engineers from December 2025" and "consumed the company's annual AI budget by April 2026" [P1 · Step 1 and preamble]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that the ranges can be multiplied out to check the claim that four months consumed an annual budget, when the problem states neither the budget's size nor the number of engineers the ranges apply to, and adoption moved from 32 percent to 84 percent during the period, so any reader who does the arithmetic gets a figure with no stated denominator.
Proposed remedy: "The reporting does not state the size of the annual AI budget or the number of engineers to which these ranges apply, so the figures cannot be combined to reproduce the overrun."

B-15. Rank 15 [Part I cumulative case, Klarna box]
Sentence: "The company reported that the assistant had handled 2.3 million conversations, accounting for two-thirds of its customer-service chats."
Conflicts with: "In February 2024, the payments company Klarna announced results from the first month of an AI assistant powered by OpenAI." [same box]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that two-thirds of Klarna's customer-service chats is a standing share of the assistant's work, when the box attaches the period explicitly only to the next statistic, "during that month"; the same box labels the 700 agents and the $40 million as estimates, so the unmarked share is the one figure a reader will carry forward as a durable capability. Klarna appears nowhere else in the chapter, so no later reuse amplifies this, but the case returns in Chapter 6.
Proposed remedy: "The company reported that, during that first month, the assistant had handled 2.3 million conversations, accounting for two-thirds of the customer-service chats handled in that month."

B-16. Rank 16 [Problems P1 · Step 5]
Sentence: "Engineering ran the rollout, and someone set the budget."
Conflicts with: "For each function performed or partly performed, name who performs it. Write 'not named' where the evidence identifies no one." [Craft section, Step 5]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that the audit located an owner for planning and budgeting, when the budget's existence proves only that some person set it; the step then correctly writes "not named" for the comparison task, so the sentence reads as an owner having been found for the setting task, which is the opposite of what the chapter's fifth function requires of a named responsible person.
Proposed remedy: "Engineering ran the rollout. The public account does not name who set the budget, so the audit records 'not named' for setting it as well as for comparing spending with it."

B-17. Rank 17 [3.4 Five questions the discipline exists to answer]
Sentence: "It can always produce an answer by asking the provider, estimating, or repeating the business case."
Conflicts with: "The public record provided no answer to three of the five Founding Questions and only part of an answer to a fourth." [Craft section, Lindy summary]
A reader would wrongly conclude: that answers are always obtainable in the sense the audit needs, and that a gap in an audit reflects only a failure to look; "always" also implies that practitioners typically do consult providers, estimates, and old business cases, a frequency claim the chapter supports for no organization it examines.
Proposed remedy: "A candidate answer can always be produced by asking the provider, estimating, or repeating the business case, and each such answer must be checked against records before it counts."

B-18. Rank 18 [3.2, fourth function, Microsoft box]

B-19. Rank 19 [Category 5 sweep: statements about what organizations generally do]
Sentence: "Managers act on claims about how their businesses work." [3.1]
Conflicts with: none
A reader would wrongly conclude: nothing serious for this sentence, and the same for the definitional or normative generics, which restate the discipline rather than report behavior: "A budget assumes that costs follow usage," "A pricing negotiation assumes that the provider has a reason to accept a lower price," "A dashboard assumes that the information it displays will change someone's decisions" [3.1]; "Two managers can hold opposing beliefs about the same deployment" [3.1]; "A manager who still budgeted the licenses as a fixed software expense" [3.1]; "Each function is a capability an organization either has or lacks" [3.2]; "The organization must state how much AI it expects to consume" [3.2]; "These choices occur even when no one makes them deliberately" [3.2]; "An organization that buys one model for all its work has still made a sourcing decision, whether or not anyone made it deliberately" [3.2]; "only it knows which team, product, or customer a request served" [3.2]; "An organization that manages its AI consumption can answer five questions from its own records" [3.4]; "An activity belongs to FinOps when it answers a question about the bill" [3.3]; "It uses AI to watch systems," "It ships models," "It asks whether a use is permitted" [Table 3.1, behavioral present tense about whole fields]; "A public record shows only what a company chose to report" [Craft, Step 4].
The three generics that do carry a wrong conclusion are reported above: "Managers can then trace a change in spending to a change in one of those drivers while there is still time to act" [3.2, a capability claim stated as what managers do, reported inside B-12's scope problem because the Pigment box is its only evidence]; "Setting the boundary in advance prevents those conducting the evaluation from choosing the version they prefer" [3.2, a claim about evaluator conduct that no quoted record supports, reported inside B-7]; and "An evaluation that counts the assistant's subscription but excludes staff time spent checking its answers reports a higher return than one that counts both" [3.2, arithmetic about a constructed case, correctly labelled and therefore clean].
Proposed remedy: "Managers often act on claims about how their businesses work, and this chapter is about the claims that can be checked." For the tracing sentence: "A manager who has stated the drivers in the plan can trace a change in spending to a change in one of them, and so act while there is still time." For the evaluator sentence: "Setting the boundary in advance removes the choice of boundary from those who run the evaluation after the results are known."
