# Northmoor: The Year in 400 Words (CFO Briefing)

We spent about $345k on AI this year across five workflows and
three providers. A year ago we could not have told you that number by team. Now
we can, and the discipline that produced it is the point of this briefing.

Three facts define the year. First, spend is concentrated: marketing content and
supplier review together account for most of the bill, while the highest-volume
workflow, inquiry triage, is nearly free. Volume is not cost, and treating them as
the same is how budgets get surprised. Second, the third quarter ran 62% over plan.
That overage was not one thing. A healthy wholesale surge from the account
expansion explains a small slice. A silent marketing template change that tripled
tokens on nearly half its tasks explains most of it. An engineering test loop left
running against a production key explains the rest. One cause was growth. Two were
failures we could have caught with a meter and an alarm. We now have both.

Third, we tested a vendor claim rather than booking it. The customer-service
assistant was sold as a 30% productivity gain. After removing a size-guide
overhaul that cut contacts and the effect of two departed agents, the defensible
gain is closer to 12%. Netted against its fully loaded cost, including review
labor, error incidents, and governance, the assistant still pays for itself, but
at a fraction of the headline. A real number we can defend beats a large number we
cannot.

The maturity we reached is not sophistication. It is attribution, a plan we can
check against actuals, and the habit of netting claims before we believe them.
Next year's target is the same discipline applied earlier, so the next 62% quarter
is a forecast, not a surprise.
